## A central involution at every width

Put C=((1+i)I+(1-i)X)/2 on one bit. At width f, put Q=C^(tensor f)
and X_f=X^(tensor f). Then Q^2=X_f and X_f^2=I. Define

    Pi_+=(I+X_f)/2, Pi_-=(I-X_f)/2,
    G=Pi_+-i Pi_-, S=Q G.

All these operators commute. Since G^2=X_f, S^2=I. Also Q^-1 G^-1=S.
The operator G is ordinary: its kernel has two entries in each row. On each
address pair (a,a xor all_ones), its matrix is

    [[(1-i)/2, (1+i)/2], [(1+i)/2, (1-i)/2]].

It has a two-ADD, two-SCALE implementation on that pair. Apply the upper
shear with coefficient i, the diagonal with entries (1-i)/2 and 1-i,
then the lower shear with coefficient i. The inverse changes i to -i.
This uses no recursive Gaussian and no extra independent input. Address
movement and scalar work still enter the ordinary compiler allowance.

G is defined from the whole X_f. It is not the tensor power of G at width
one. At width one S=I. At width two S is not I. In the Fourier basis, a
character of weight w has S eigenvalue (-1)^(w(w-1)/2). This distinction
prevents a one-column identity from being lifted by an invalid tensor rule.
The displayed operator algebra proves the identity for every positive f.

## An advancing stage

Let W be the product of the eight diagonal phase gates diag(1,i), on the
active eight-axis block. For the canonical eight-axis quarter transform D,

    W D W = H_8.

There is no omitted phase. On one axis the phase is exp(i*pi/4); eight axes
give phase one. The same identity holds in each tensor column. The exact
integer check compares all 65536 entries on the eight-axis block.

Use three disjoint eight-axis blocks. Let R cycle them and let H_0 act on
the first block. Put U=R H_0. Then U has order six. U^2 contains two distinct
Hadamard blocks followed by R^2; U^3 contains all three blocks. Thus U^2 S
and U^3 S are ordinary equivalents of Gaussian jobs of ranks17 and25.
This statement includes the inverse G target adapter. It does not price a
word that has not been constructed.

## Bind one full call

The retained source interface is D[[1,0],[Q^-1,1]], with 104 independent
port pairs and fresh independent helpers. Its lower shear is a source
result, credited to the P104/native/controller lineage. Use these main-only
operations, in execution order, for each port:

1. Apply x <- x-y.
2. Apply G^-1 to y, then W to both main fields.
3. Swap the two main fields, run the unchanged forward source, and swap back.
4. Apply W to both fields, G to y, then R to both fields.

The output is

    (x,y) -> (U x-U(1-S)y, U y).

The source helpers retain their original inputs and outputs. The source
COPY and CLEAR operations are unchanged. There are two swaps, one ADD,
four W maps, two G maps and two R maps per port, besides the source routes.
They require an explicit ordinary-work allowance in a completed package.
No numerical search receipt is a replay of these source routes.

## Frames with coefficients in R

Let A be the commutative algebra generated by R, with R^3=I. A constant
query basis puts its rank-one nilpotent in the form E_01. For x,y over A
and c over A, define

    C = [[1,c,-y], [0,1,0], [0,x,I]],
    C^-1 = [[1,-c-y*x,y], [0,1,0], [0,-x,I]].

These are exact inverses. Both are products of ordinary field shears whose
coefficients have at most three permutation terms. Conjugate the full call
by C and by the constant query basis. This gives the formulas in
permutation_frame_search.py and permutation_frame_numeric.py. The constant
term of c cancels because it commutes with U; it can be fixed to zero.

The binary search works in F2[(C2)^3 semidirect C3], including every group
coefficient. Its positive two-field control passes a separate literal
integer replay. The characteristic-zero search uses all six scalar and both
three-dimensional irreducible representations. Floating residuals do not
establish a group-algebra identity.
